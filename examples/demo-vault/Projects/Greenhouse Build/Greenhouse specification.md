# Greenhouse specification

Working specification for the lean-to greenhouse. Everything here is my own choice; the contractor builds the base and the frame, I do the glazing and the inside.

## Dimensions

- Footprint 3.0 m along the shed wall by 4.0 m out from it.
- Eaves at 1.9 m on the outer wall, ridge at 2.6 m against the shed.
- Door on the east end, 0.8 m wide, opening outward.
- Soil bed 0.9 m wide along the outer wall, 0.45 m deep, edged with sleepers.
- Staging along the shed wall, 0.6 m deep, two tiers.

## Structure

- Base: 100 mm concrete slab on 100 mm compacted hardcore, with a 150 mm upstand around the edge so the frame sits above splash height.
- Frame: 75 by 50 treated softwood at 600 mm centres, sill plate bolted to the upstand with M10 resin anchors.
- Rafters: 100 by 50 at 600 mm centres, birdsmouthed onto the wall plate, hung on joist hangers at the shed wall.
- Ridge fixed to the shed with three M12 through-bolts into the shed's studs, not into the cladding.

## Glazing

- Twin-wall polycarbonate, 10 mm, clear, UV-coated face out.
- Held in H-profile aluminium bars with rubber gaskets; sheets sealed at the top with aluminium tape and at the bottom with breather tape.
- Roof pitch 15 degrees, which is enough for run-off and not so steep that the sheets creep.

## Ventilation

See [[Ventilation plan]]. Two roof vents on wax openers, one louvre in the door end, one louvre low on the west end for cross-draught.

## Water

- Water butt on the shed downpipe feeding a hose with a timer.
- Drip line along the bed, 2 L/h emitters at 300 mm.
- See [[Watering schedule v3]] for timings by season.

## Build sequence

1. Mark out 3.2 by 4.2 m with pegs and string; check the diagonals to 5 mm.
2. Dig out 250 mm; barrow the soil to the far corner for the bed.
3. Hardcore in two 50 mm layers, whacked eight passes each way, watered between.
4. Shuttering to the finished slab height; DPM over the hardcore, folded up the sides.
5. Mesh on chairs, 50 mm cover, lapped 300 mm.
6. Pour, tamp with the straightedge, float, cover with sheet; leave a week.
7. Upstand shuttering, second small pour, 150 mm high; leave three days.
8. Strip the shuttering; keep the boards for the bed edging.
9. Drill the upstand for the resin anchors; set the sill plate level with packers.
10. Studs up on the outer wall, braced temporarily; head plate on.
11. Wall plate onto the shed studs with the through-bolts; check it is level with a hose level.
12. Rafters cut from one template; hangers at the shed, birdsmouth at the eaves.
13. End walls studded to the roof line; door frame in.
14. Ridge flashing sealed to the shed cladding.
15. Glazing bars on the rafters; sheets fed in from the eaves end, UV face out.
16. Tape the sheet ends; F-trim at the eaves.
17. Vents framed, hung, openers fitted and set.
18. Door made, hung, planed to fit.
19. Sleepers in; bed filled; drip line laid and timed.
20. Staging built from the offcuts; thermometer hung; first plants in.

## Snag list, with what was done

- door binds at the top: planed 3 mm, fine since
- west louvre sticks when damp: nylon washer on the pivot, still sticks a little
- one glazing bar rattles in wind: a dab of sealant under the cap
- drip line kinks at the bed corner: a short stake to hold the curve
- thermometer read 3° high in sun: moved it under the staging
- condensation drips from the ridge on cold mornings: a strip of foam under the flashing
- wax opener on the east vent slow to close: cleaned the piston
- sill plate weeping at one anchor: sealant, then dry
- slab low corner: packers under the sill, invisible now
- polycarbonate creeps 2 mm in heat: the F-trim allows it, nothing to do
- compost bins too close to the door: moved again
- hose timer battery dies in frost: bring it in for the winter
- the staging is 50 mm too high for the tomato pots: a lower rail added
- birds nesting in the ridge gap: mesh over the gap
- slugs under the sleepers: copper tape, which half works
- algae on the north face of the sheets: a wash in spring
- the door latch rattles: a felt pad
- the east louvre lets rain in sideways: a small hood
- vent openers over-open in a gale: a chain limiter
- mouse in the seed tray: a tin
